`gc --keep N` keeps each install path's `N` newest generations that `rollback --to` can switch back to, however old they are; the other trees are removed past their grace period as before. `gc --check-in-use`, before it removes a tree or a run's generation directory, with or without `--dry-run`, looks in `/proc` for a use of it on this node and keeps one in use, naming the mount or the process: a mount at or inside it, a bind mount of it in any mount namespace, or a process that runs a program from it, has its working or root directory in it, or holds open or maps a file in it. Without the option `gc` reads nothing in `/proc`, as before. A tree that `--keep` or this check keeps leaves its run's generation directory in place too. Other nodes, and without root other users' processes, are not seen; the grace period still protects them.
